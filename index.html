Endalkachew Abebe Kebede

Postdoctoral Research Associate, University of Delaware. I map where and when the world irrigates, and what that means for water and food.

I’m Endi, a hydrologist and geospatial data scientist working where water, agriculture, and global food systems meet. I study how irrigation, climate variability, and land-use change reshape freshwater availability and the sustainability of food production, from single river basins to the whole planet.

My work combines hydrological modeling, remote sensing, machine learning, and open geospatial data. A central contribution is MIRCA-OS, an open global dataset of monthly irrigated and rainfed cropland for the 21st century. I also lead GMIA-NEXT, a 30 m global irrigation map, and contribute to HarvestStat, an international effort to standardize subnational crop statistics.

I trained in engineering and geospatial science across Africa, Asia, Europe, and the United States, and taught and worked as an engineer in Ethiopia for over a decade before my PhD. I care about teaching, mentoring, and open science, and about research communities that connect data to real decisions.
